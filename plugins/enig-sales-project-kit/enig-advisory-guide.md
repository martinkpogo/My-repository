# ENIG ADVISORY GUIDE

**Status:** Canonical. Approved by Martin, 24 Nov 2026 (LOG-1359). Used with `enig-design-sop.md`. **Prices come only from the SOP** (§2, §2A–§2C, §4); this guide never sets a price. Canonical text: the "ENIG Advisory Guide" Notion page under Isolated Sales: Operating Specification. This file is the Claude Project's working copy and must match it.

## 1. Purpose

After Martin's conversation with a client: assess what the client needs (the tier), quote what they asked for, and recommend what would serve their **result** better, tied to their business, with the consequences of each choice. Martin decides; the client chooses. A recommendation is never a condition.

## 2. The six-step conversation

**Goal → Why now → Proof → Stakes → Practicals → Next.**

| Step | Ask | Listen for |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | "What do you want this to achieve for the business?" | A visual goal, or a business result |
| Why now | "What made you decide to do this now?" | A launch, or a problem |
| Proof | "What tells you that? What have customers or shops said?" | Evidence, or only belief |
| Stakes | "What happens if it doesn't work? Roughly what's riding on it?" | Low, or high |
| Practicals | Items, deadline, files, printing, who approves | The brief |
| Next | "I'll send you your options by {day}." | — |

Steps 1–4 point to the tier; step 5 is the brief. Never promise a price on the call. From level 3, Martin may give the anchor (SOP §1).

## 3. Question bank (choose by enquiry)

Pick the modules that fit the request, the situation and the client type. Drop what the client has already said. Put the 3–4 most important questions first.

**By request**

- **Logo:** what the business does and for whom · what is wrong with the current one · brands they admire and why · where it will be used most.
- **Labels and packaging:** how many products, flavours and sizes · shared container · where sold and what sits next to it on the shelf · regulatory text (ingredients, FDA) · who prints and the run size.
- **Social media posts:** platforms · what each post is for · photos and copy · how often they post now and who makes the posts.
- **Flyers, posters, banners:** where seen and by whom · what people should do after seeing it · the date · print or digital, and quantity.
- **Reports, profiles, editorial:** who reads it · page count · is the text final · charts or photos · approvers and rounds.
- **Rebrand or identity:** what has changed in the business · what people should think or feel differently · who decides · what must stay.
- **Signage, vehicles, uniforms:** how many · who produces them · rollout plan and budget.

**By situation**

- **Launch:** date · where and to whom · what success looks like in numbers.
- **Entering retail:** which shops · has any said no, and why · what competitors on that shelf look like.
- **A business problem:** since when · what customers have said · what they have already tried · what else changed at that time (price, staff, competitors).
- **Tender or pitch:** deadline and value · what the buyer needs to see · who else is pitching.
- **Growth:** new locations or markets · new places the brand must work (vehicles, uniforms, packaging).
- **"We just want it to look better":** better than what or whom · who noticed · what would change if it did.
- **Tight deadline:** what is fixed and what can come in a second phase.

**By client type**

- **Micro or very small:** keep it short and practical; mind their budget.
- **Established:** who approves, how many people are involved, existing brand rules.
- **Large:** procurement, approvals, timelines, existing guidelines or agencies.

**Always, when a business result comes up**

- **Evidence for what they believe:** "What makes you think the design is the reason?" · "What have customers or shops actually said?" · "What have you tried, and what happened?"
- **Figures** (naturally): outlets or shops, orders or enquiries a month, what one customer or shop is worth, the cost of a print run, current spend, what success means in numbers.
- **Budget**, gently, with ranges: "Is there a rough range you're working with, for example under GHS 5,000, 5,000–15,000, or more?"

## 4. Tiers: what is already settled

Every job has a business reason. The tier depends on what is already settled.

| Tier | Settled | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Delivery** | The goal **and** the solution, and the solution fits the reason | A brief and a quote |
| **B. Options** | The goal and a cause good enough to act on; the best solution is open | A brief, the quote for what they asked, and a recommended path with trade-offs |
| **C. Investigate** | A real business problem whose cause is only assumed, and costly to get wrong | The quote for what they asked, and a recommendation to discover first |

Three questions: Have they decided what to make, and does it fit their reason? (A) · Is the cause good enough to act on? (B) · Is it weak and costly to get wrong? (C)

## 5. Hypothesis test

The cause the client believes in is a **hypothesis (HYP)**, not a fact.

| Check | Points to B (act on it) | Points to C (investigate) |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence | Customer or shop feedback, numbers, things tried | Only their belief |
| Consistency | The facts agree | Contradictions |
| Specificity | A clear audience and cause | Unsure who buys, or several possible causes |
| Cost of being wrong | Low | High: print runs, retail, rollouts |
| Reversibility | Easy to adjust | Hard to undo: packaging, signage, vehicles |

Show the scoring to Martin. **Judge the evidence; never diagnose.** Diagnosis belongs to Strategy through the consultancy route.

## 6. Level signals

The client's own prices · premises · customers · outlets and markets · print volumes and media spend · approvers and procurement · how they present themselves now. The level is set by scale or usage, whichever is higher (SOP §2A). If unclear, say so and ask; Martin sets the level.

## 7. Budget and anchoring

| Budget compared with the Floor | Do |
|---|---|
| Well above | Check the level; offer the full path. Never price up to the budget. |
| About the same | A good fit. |
| Below | A shorter or phased path, at the same prices. |
| Far below the shortest path | A polite decline or a small first step (Martin's choice). |
| Not given | Price from the Floor and value as usual; ask gently. |

**Anchoring (level 3 and above):** "Projects like this usually start from X." The client's reaction qualifies them; it is not a pricing input.

## 8. The six-part message

1. **I hear you:** their result and request, in their words.
2. **I can do it:** what they asked for, its price and delivery time.
3. **I'd recommend…:** a fuller path, a brand system or a retainer, or discovery first (tier C).
4. **…because:** two or three reasons, each tied to something they said.
5. **If you stick with your request, this could happen:** two or three likely consequences from what they told you. "Could", not "will".
6. **Your choice:** "Either way, we're happy to do it."

- **Tier A:** just the quote. Never invent a reason to upsell.
- **Maths:** only figures the client gave; at most one comparison. No figures, no maths.
- **Complex work:** show the value they agreed first, then the path, then the price.
- **Tier C, if they decline discovery:** design to their hypothesis, recorded as "Brief based on the client's hypothesis (HYP, not verified): {their words}", and add to the quote: "This design responds to the brief you gave us. We haven't looked into the cause of {their goal}, so we can't promise the new design alone will change it."
- **At delivery:** always recommend a retainer for consistency (Content Plan or Brand Care): "Without it, new posts, flyers and printer files tend to drift from the brand over time."

## 9. The offer card (for Martin, before any client message)

- **Facts:** the brief, open gaps, deadline, printing.
- **Level:** with the evidence.
- **Tier:** A, B or C, with the hypothesis-test scoring for B and C.
- **What they asked for:** the line-by-line price (SOP §4).
- **Recommended path:** its steps and Floor.
- **Shorter path:** its steps, Floor and trade-off.
- **Value at stake:** each figure with its source and evidence quality; the ratio; the 5–15% sanity check.
- **Budget fit:** above, about the same, below or far below.
- **Consequences and maths:** tied to the conversation.
- **Draft message:** the six-part structure.
- **Records to write:** `Engagement_Type`, `Level`, `Result` = Open.

## 10. Examples

| They say | Tier | Recommend |
|---|---|---|
| "A flyer for our church event on Sunday." | A | Just the quote |
| "A logo for our new bakery, warm and homely." | A | Just the quote |
| "I need a logo but I'm not sure what style suits me." | B | Brand Starter |
| "We want to look premium so shops take us seriously." | B | Brand System |
| "Shops told us our labels look cheaper than competitors'." | B | Brand System (evidence supports it) |
| "Shops won't stock us. We think it's our logo." | C | Discovery first |
| "We've redesigned twice and nothing changed." | C | Discovery first |
| "We're like every caterer; customers pick on price." | C | Discovery first |
| "10 posts for our sale next week." | A | The quote; a Content Plan at delivery |

## 11. Never

Invent reasons, figures or offers · diagnose · let budget raise the price · show the formula · make a recommendation a condition.
